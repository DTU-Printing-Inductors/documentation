## Suggested structure

**1. Research depth: how much of *their* offering you've looked at** (you already have this)

- 1 - Website skimmed

- 3 - Full catalogue searched

- 0 - Not contacted
- 1 - Inquiry sent (no reply yet)
- 2 - Replied (they answered at least once)
- 3 - In discussion (back and forth: quotes, samples, specs)

- Waiting on me
- Closed

- With two ordinal axes you can make a base view grouped by one and sorted by the other. That shows gaps at a glance, like high research depth with no contact (Toda Kogyo, ALBIS) or deep talks with a shallow search.

**ai**: How well a TPE bonds to these plastics depends mostly on **which kind of TPE** it is, because "TPE" covers several chemically different families. KERAMOLD's catalogue only says "silicone-free TPE". Its Shore A hardness and a melt-flow test at 170 °C suggest a styrenic TPE (SEBS), but that's my guess and the catalogue doesn't confirm it.

## How the bond forms
Overmolding or depositing a TPE onto a rigid plastic bonds through up to four mechanisms:
1. **Chain interdiffusion:** polymer chains from both sides tangle at the interface. This only happens if the two are compatible and the interface gets hot enough.
2. **Polar interactions and hydrogen bonds:** for example urethane groups (TPU) with amide groups (polyamides).
3. **Chemical reaction:** "PA-bondable" TPS grades carry maleic-anhydride groups that react with the amine end groups of polyamides. This is how SEBS is made to stick to nylon.
4. **Mechanical interlock:** undercuts, holes and ribs. This is the fallback when the chemistry doesn't work.

## By substrate
| Substrate | TPU | Standard SEBS (TPS) | Adhesion-modified TPS | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **PA6** | Good | Poor | Good | Many amide groups, so the most bondable. Slightly more forgiving than PA66. |
| **PA66** | Good | Poor | Good | Similar to PA6. Needs higher interface temperatures because PA66 melts around 260 °C. |
| **PA12** | Moderate | Poor | Moderate | Fewer amide groups per chain, so weaker polar bonding. Sources recommend interlock features or primers. PEBA (polyether-block-amide, often PA12-based) is chemically the closest match. |
| **POM** | Poor–moderate | Poor | Special grades exist | Low surface energy and chemically inert. Usually relies on mechanical interlock. |

Without adhesion promotion, standard SEBS has little affinity for polyamides, and results range from marginal to inadequate ([Incure](https://incurelab.com/wp/?p=15653)). Silane coupling agents or adhesion-modified grades are the usual fixes ([Incure](https://incurelab.com/wp/?p=15652)). For POM, mechanical retention is the standard approach ([Incure](https://incurelab.com/wp/?p=15706)), though specially formulated TPE compounds with better POM adhesion do exist ([EPO filing](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP1225036NWA1/document.pdf)).

## Process factors
- **Moisture** in the polyamide or the TPE weakens the bond, so dry both ([Incure](https://incurelab.com/wp/?p=15658), [UL Prospector / Teknor Apex](https://www.ulprospector.com/knowledge/media/2021/08/Overmolding-with-TPEs-webinar-slides-September-2021.pdf)).
- **A higher TPE melt temperature** gives a stronger bond ([UL Prospector / Teknor Apex](https://www.ulprospector.com/knowledge/media/2021/08/Overmolding-with-TPEs-webinar-slides-September-2021.pdf)).
- **For pellet printing:** if KERAMOLD processes at around 170–200 °C, which is my inference from the melt-flow condition, depositing it onto a cooled PA6 or PA66 layer won't re-melt the polyamide surface. You'd get little interdiffusion and a weak interface. Printing the polyamide onto the TPE gives more heat at the interface, but risks degrading the TPE. A heated chamber or bed would help in either case.
