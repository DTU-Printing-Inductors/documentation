Soft magnetic cores are used in many inductors and transformers to guide magnetic flux and influence their electromagnetic performance~\cite{valchevInductorsTransformersPower2018}. The performance of these devices is heavily influenced by the distribution of magnetic flux within the core material. A nonuniform magnetic flux distribution lead to higher core losses~\cite{aghebModificationEmpiricalCore2013} and higher localized saturation~\cite{umetaniImprovementSaturationProperty2013}. Therefore, uniform magnetic flux distribution is desired.

Can pellet extrusion produce a controlled permeability gradient?
This is the key technical bridge in your argument.
- Has anyone independently metered magnetic-composite and unfilled pellets during a single print?
- Can their mixing ratio be varied continuously, and how quickly does the extrudate respond to a change in feed ratio?
- Does the screw provide sufficient distributive mixing, or are additional mixing features required?
- How uniform is the magnetic-particle distribution within the extrudate?
- How does pellet feed ratio translate into actual particle concentration and effective permeability?
- What are the minimum achievable gradient length, transition width and repeatability?



Why vary composition instead of geometry?
Your most important justification question.
- Can a continuous composition gradient achieve a magnetic response that is difficult to approximate using discrete geometric regions?
- Is the desired gradient finer than the spatial resolution of the printing process?
- Can composition grading avoid adding multiple magnetic materials or complex internal geometries?
- Does changing composition provide a useful degree of freedom beyond geometry, or can geometry achieve the same performance with less complexity?
- Under equal volume, material and operating constraints, which approach produces the best core performance?



What is actually missing from the literature?
Define a defensible research gap.
- Has pellet extrusion already been used to produce spatially graded magnetic composites?
- Has the local permeability of such a printed gradient been measured?
- Has a graded pellet-extruded core been tested as an inductor?
- Has its performance been compared against a uniform core and a geometry-optimised core?
- If these demonstrations are missing, is the missing contribution the manufacturing process, the magnetic characterisation, the core design or the device-level performance?
