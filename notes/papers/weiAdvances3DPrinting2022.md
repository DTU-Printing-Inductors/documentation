## Theory

**Ferromagnets -** strongly attracted to a magnetic field, all fields alligned in the same direction (Iron, Nickel, Steel)

**Ferrimagnets -** strongly attracted to a magnetic field, some in the same direction and some in the opposite direction (Magnetite Fe3O4)

**Paramagnets -** weakly attracted to a magnetic field (Aluminium, platinum)

**Diamagnets -** weakly repelled by a magnetic field (Gold, copper)

![alt text](/images/type_of_magnetic_materials.png)

**Bonded magnets -** magnets, that are formed with adhesives, usually polymers. Must have high coercivity to avoid demagnetization and enable operation at high temperatures.

**Sintered magnets -** created with sintering (with heat and pressure go from powder to solid)

Performance of bonded magnets with the same volume is far inferior to that of sintered magnets

4 main indicators for measuring the performance of permanent magnets:

* remanence

* coercivity

* intrinsic coercivity

* maximum energy product (BH)max.

Soft magnets are commonly used in inductors and trasformers, due to their low coercivity, thus being easily demagnetizable.

**Anisotropic** - physical properties that change depending on the direction of measurement
**Isotropic** - physical properties do NOT change depending on the direction of measurment
